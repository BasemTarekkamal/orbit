# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A solo founder or professional on a Mac or a Windows or Linux PC, usually with one or two AI employees, sometimes a small
team with several. They open the control panel a few times a day: in the morning to see what their
assistant did overnight and what needs them, during the day to give an order or hire someone, in the
evening to read the end-of-day report. They are not developers; Terminal is for setup only.

## Product Purpose

Orbit turns a person's own Claude Code into AI employees that work on their own computer: research,
routines, memory, chat (Telegram, Slack or Discord), skills they learn. The control panel is where the owner sees the team, directs it and tunes
what each employee may do. Success: the owner trusts the panel at a glance (what's running, what
needs me, what did it cost), reaches any action in one or two clicks, and would show it on a screen
when announcing the project.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, capable. The voice of a good chief of staff: short sentences, concrete facts, no
cheerleading. The interface feels like a serious Mac tool (Linear, Raycast): dense where data lives,
quiet elsewhere, native in both light and dark appearance.

## Anti-references

- The generic SaaS dashboard: big-number tiles, gradients, identical card grids, "insights" copy.
- The chatbot wrapper: one chat box and a sidebar of conversations.
- Enterprise admin tables with no hierarchy, and anything that looks like a template.

## Design Principles

- **What needs me, first.** The home view leads with decisions and blockers, then with what happened.
- **One vocabulary.** The same button, select, list and badge everywhere; status shown the same way on
  every screen.
- **Numbers are honest.** Plan use is labelled as plan use, never as a bill; failures are shown, not
  smoothed over.
- **Density where it earns it.** Lists and the abilities matrix are dense; the home view breathes.
- **Nothing to learn.** Standard Mac conventions, keyboard-friendly, readable empty states that say
  what to do next.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA: body text ≥ 4.5:1, visible focus rings, every control labelled, no information carried by
color alone (status always has a word), reduced-motion alternatives for every transition, works at
phone width.
